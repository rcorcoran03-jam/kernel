Lunisa Arts

A founder-led summer art camp in Minneapolis, given its operating structure from the ground up, then a new master brand that opens the door to the founder’s wider art, with one back office that runs registration, families, forms and the camp day behind both.

The challenge

A new camp with a clear creative vision needed the business underneath it: defined offerings and prices, a way to enroll families, and a rhythm beyond summer. Once camp was running, the work still lived in too many places: registrations in the inbox, forms on paper, payments in notes, pickups on a clipboard, and no way to tell which posts or flyers filled weeks. And the founder’s art reaches well beyond a kids’ camp, with no home for the rest of her work.

What we built
  • Offering structure and pricing for camp, small-group lessons, birthday and private parties, plus a recurring events framework
  • A new master brand for the founder’s studio, with Lunisa as its camp, making room for adult workshops, commissions and her own art
  • Studio Desk, a built-in CRM: enrollments and waitlist, a record for every family, camp weeks that update the public site, and one inquiry board for both brands
  • Online forms with e-signatures that freeze the exact text signed, plus automatic reminders
  • The Today Board: tablet check-in, checkout only to approved adults, headcounts that name who’s missing, and incident reports parents acknowledge
  • The digital front doors: the live camp site today, and new sites for both brands on the same database, with private family pages
  • Online payments with deposits, discounts, promo codes and refunds, or pay on arrival, all in one ledger; lead-source reporting next (planned)
  • A staff training video, one short chapter per task, so new helpers learn check-in, headcounts and pickups before their first camp day

How it works

A family registers once and gets a private page for forms and pickups, with no account or password. The founder confirms a spot in one click, and the confirmation email handles the follow-up. On camp days, staff run the day from one screen with allergies and support notes in view, and every check-in, payment and incident lands back in Studio Desk. New helpers learn the day from the training video and can rewatch any chapter. The camp site is live today. Studio Desk runs as a working preview with demo data and replaces the inbox once the founder signs off.

How we build

Six principles, each with a guardrail.

Whether it’s AI or a simple form, these are the rules we build by, so you always know what a tool can do, what it can’t, and who’s in charge. (You are.)

  1. 01Built around what already works.

    We start with how the work actually moves and the tools you already pay for, then build only the missing rooms.

    GuardrailYour shop, marketplace or payment provider keeps doing what it does well, if it’s doing it well. We connect rather than replace, to minimize disruption.

  2. 02AI drafts. You decide.

    AI is good at first drafts: replies, descriptions, summaries, sorting. You stay the editor and the final word.

    GuardrailNothing is sent, posted, paid or listed on your behalf unless you approve it, or you’ve switched that on for one specific, low‑stakes task.

  3. 03Your rules, in plain settings.

    Prices, discounts, deposits, refund windows and dates live as settings you can change yourself. AI can suggest changes, but it never makes them.

    GuardrailA reminder can’t move a delivery date you promised, and no discount can go past the limit you set.

  4. 04Test first, with an easy way back.

    Every build starts as a private preview with sample data and test payments. AI features are checked against your own past examples before they touch a customer.

    GuardrailNothing reaches a real customer until you’ve walked through it yourself, and every switch can be undone. Your old site stays live until you say go.

  5. 05Everyone sees only what they need.

    Staff, admins and customers each get their own view. Your accounts sit on business plans that don’t train on your data by default.

    GuardrailHelpers see an allergy flag, not the medical note. Costs and margins stay in your back office. AI never makes or imitates your creative work.

  6. 06A record you can trust.

    Every change notes who made it and when, and every customer has one history instead of five scattered threads.

    GuardrailSigned forms keep the exact text that was signed, and incident reports can be added to but never quietly rewritten.

Questions

Good things to ask before you start.

These are the questions we hear most, especially the worried ones. If yours isn’t here, bring it to the follow‑up call.

About AI itself

Will it send things or make decisions on my behalf?

Not unless you choose that; see principle 02. If something routine, like a booking confirmation with fixed wording, should go out on its own, we switch it on for that one task only, show you how to turn it off, and keep a record of what was sent.

Is my information safe? What about security?

Each person and tool gets only the access it needs; see principle 05. Some free and personal AI plans do train on your data unless you change a setting, so we check those together. Passwords and sensitive client details stay out of AI tools, and you own every account and can close it any time.

Will AI use or copy my creative work?

Not in anything we build. In Discovery we also check the tools you already use, including image tools, for settings that let them learn from what you upload, and switch those off where you can.

Isn’t using AI putting other people out of work?

It’s a fair concern, and we take it seriously. Our work is aimed at the tasks you’re already doing yourself after hours: the inbox, the admin, the half‑finished website. The goal is to give time back to you and your team, not to replace someone you’d otherwise hire. We won’t scope a project whose purpose is cutting a person’s job.

What about the energy and data centers behind AI?

It’s a real cost. Data centers are using a growing share of the world’s electricity, and AI is a big reason why. We keep your footprint small on purpose: the smallest tool that does the job, plain rules instead of AI wherever they work, and no image or video generation unless it’s actually needed. Using AI for a few useful tasks a week is very different from running it all day.

Should I be worried about where AI is heading?

Serious people debate AI’s long‑term risks, and it’s reasonable to follow that conversation. What we set up is much more ordinary: tools that draft, sort, and summarize under your supervision. We don’t build systems that act on their own without you, and everything comes with an off switch.

I don’t trust it yet. Do I have to?

No. Trust should be earned, the same way you came to rely on a spreadsheet. Every build starts as a preview you walk through yourself (principle 04), then begins with low‑stakes tasks where you check every result. It does more only when you’re comfortable. You decide the pace.

What if it gets something wrong?

It will, sometimes. That’s why you approve what matters and why your rules live in settings AI can’t change. When it misses, that case becomes a new test, so the same mistake gets caught next time.

What will the AI tools cost me each month?

Separate from our fees, most businesses start with tools they already pay for or a single paid plan; individual plans from the major providers are about the price of a streaming subscription, and team plans with stronger data protection cost a little more per person. Before we build anything, you see the expected monthly cost in writing. We avoid tools that bill by usage without a spending cap, so there are no surprise bills.

How do I keep it running after we leave?

That’s the point of “teach one.” Everything is built in accounts you own, with a short plain‑language guide for each build: what it does, where it lives, how to change it, and how to turn it off. By hand‑off, you or a teammate has already made changes without us. If you want a check‑in later, we can do that, but you won’t need us to keep it working.
